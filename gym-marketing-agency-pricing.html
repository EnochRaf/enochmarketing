HOW GYM MARKETING AGENCIES STRUCTURE THEIR PRICING

Most agencies fall into one of four pricing structures. The structure matters as much as the number, because the same monthly fee can mean very different things depending on what's actually included.

FLAT-RATE MONTHLY RETAINERS

You pay a fixed amount each month regardless of how much work gets done or how many leads come in. For gym owners, this is the most predictable model — you can budget it the same way you budget rent or software.

The range in 2026 is wide. Entry-level retainers for basic local SEO or social posting start around $500 to $800 per month. Full-funnel retainers from agencies handling ads, SEO, website, and lead follow-up typically run $1,500 to $5,000 per month before ad spend.

The key question with any flat-rate retainer: does ad spend come out of that fee, or is it billed on top? A lot of agencies bundle a small ad budget into a low monthly rate and call it all-inclusive, but the actual spend is too thin to move the needle. Always confirm what the fee covers and what gets charged separately.

PERCENTAGE OF AD SPEND

Some agencies charge 10 to 20 percent of whatever you're spending on paid ads each month, sometimes with a minimum floor. If you're running $3,000 per month on Meta and Google, you'd pay the agency an additional $300 to $600 on top of that.

This model ties the agency's income to the size of your ad budget, not to how many members you're actually acquiring. An agency on percentage-of-spend has a financial reason to recommend higher budgets. That's not automatically a problem, but it's worth understanding before you agree to the terms.

PROGRAM FEES AND COACHING MODELS

This is a different category entirely. Programs like Gym Launch charge $18,000 to $40,000 or more in upfront or structured fees. What you're buying is coaching and education on how to market your gym — not someone doing the marketing for you. That distinction matters enormously. If you want to learn the system and run it yourself, this model has real value. If you want someone running the funnel while you coach your members, it's the wrong tool.

Two-Brain Business operates as a 12-month mentorship program that has worked with more than 2,700 gym owners. It focuses on business advisory and education, not done-for-you execution. Paid ad management, local SEO, and website builds are not part of what they deliver.

PERFORMANCE-BASED OR HYBRID MODELS

A smaller number of agencies offer outcome-based pricing, where part of the fee is tied to a guaranteed number of leads or members. Optimized Growth, for example, advertises a guaranteed member acquisition outcome, though their pricing isn't publicly listed.

Performance models sound appealing, but the fine print matters. "Guaranteed leads" and "guaranteed members" are not the same thing. Lead quality, how fast your team follows up, and your gym's own sales process all determine whether a lead actually becomes a paying member.

WHAT'S USUALLY NOT INCLUDED IN THE MONTHLY FEE

This is where gym owners get surprised most often. The retainer number in the proposal is rarely the total cost.

Ad spend is almost always separate. A $2,500 per month agency retainer might require an additional $1,500 to $3,000 per month in Meta and Google ad spend to actually generate leads. Some agencies are upfront about this; others bury it.

Website builds or redesigns are frequently scoped as separate projects, even when "website management" appears in the retainer. Confirm whether a new site is included or quoted separately before you sign.

CRM and software subscriptions can add $100 to $400 per month. Email platforms, SMS tools, scheduling software, and landing page builders all carry their own costs.

Onboarding fees are common at mid-market agencies. A one-time setup charge of $500 to $2,000 is not unusual.

Content production beyond templates. Some agencies include done-for-you social content in their retainer. Others hand you templates and expect you to film and post. Know which one you're getting before you commit.

The Rule of Thumb

Always ask: what is my total monthly cost, including ad spend and software? The agency fee is only part of the number that shows up on your bank statement.

PRICING TIERS IN PRACTICE: WHAT EACH LEVEL GETS YOU

Here's a realistic look at what different monthly investment levels typically deliver in 2026, based on what full-service gym marketing agencies offer at each band.

$500 TO $1,200 PER MONTH

At this level, you're usually getting one service: local SEO, basic social posting, or a landing page. Execution tends to be templated, and the person managing your account may be handling 50 or more clients at the same time. There's no single point of accountability for your overall growth.

This range can make sense if you have a specific, narrow gap to fill and you're handling everything else yourself. It rarely works as a complete marketing solution.

$1,500 TO $2,500 PER MONTH

This is where full-service gym marketing agencies start to operate. At $1,500 per month, you should expect local SEO, Google Business Profile optimization, and some form of content or social support. At $2,500, paid ad management on Meta or Google should be part of the package.

Multi-location operators, franchises, and gyms with more complex needs often require custom scoping. Coaching and consulting programs like Gym Launch operate in this range with fees of $18,000 to $40,000 or more — but what you're purchasing is knowledge transfer, not execution.

THE HIDDEN COST OF FRAGMENTED MARKETING

There's a cost that never shows up on any invoice: the time you spend managing vendors who don't talk to each other.

A typical fragmented setup looks like this. A freelancer runs your Facebook ads. A separate developer maintains your website. You have a CRM that sends automated emails, but nobody set it up properly. When a lead falls through the cracks, there's no one accountable. The ads person blames the website. The developer says the CRM wasn't configured right. You end up chasing it down at 10pm after coaching the last class.

That coordination overhead has real dollar value. When you add up what you're spending across three or four vendors, plus your own time managing them, the actual cost of "cheaper" fragmented marketing often exceeds what a single full-service agency charges.

WHAT TO ASK BEFORE SIGNING ANY GYM MARKETING CONTRACT

A few questions that will tell you more than any sales call:

Is ad spend included in the monthly fee or billed separately? If it's included, what's the actual budget — and is it enough to generate meaningful volume in your market?

Who owns the ad accounts, landing pages, and website if you leave? Some agencies build everything inside their own accounts, which means you lose all the assets and history the moment you switch.

What does reporting look like, and how often do you see it? A live dashboard you can check anytime is a fundamentally different thing from a monthly PDF.

What happens in the first 90 days? Agencies that can describe a specific ramp period with defined milestones are more accountable than those with vague "we'll get started right away" timelines.

How does lead follow-up work? Speed-to-lead is one of the biggest failure points in gym marketing. A lead that doesn't hear back within five minutes is far less likely to convert. Ask whether follow-up is automated, how fast it triggers, and what happens when a lead asks something that requires a real answer.

Enoch Scout, Enoch Marketing's automated follow-up workflow, handles this by routing qualified conversations toward an intro call and escalating judgment-sensitive exchanges to the gym's own team. It's easy to overlook when you're comparing monthly fees, but it has a direct effect on how many leads actually become members.

Pre-Signing Checklist
  • Confirm whether ad spend is included in the fee or billed separately — and get the actual monthly budget in writing
  • Clarify ownership of ad accounts, landing pages, website, and CRM data if you cancel
  • Ask what reporting looks like and whether you have real-time dashboard access or monthly PDFs
  • Request a specific 90-day milestone plan with defined deliverables and check-in points
  • Understand how lead follow-up is handled — automated or manual, speed-to-lead timing, and escalation path
  • Add up all costs: agency fee + ad spend + software subscriptions + onboarding fee

IS CHEAPER ALWAYS WRONG?

Not necessarily. If you're a newer gym still building your operational foundation, a $1,500 per month local SEO engagement might be exactly the right starting point. Paying $4,500 per month when you have 60 members and $8,000 in monthly revenue doesn't make financial sense.

The math that matters is return on investment, not the absolute fee. A $2,500 per month retainer that consistently brings in four to six new members per month at an average value of $150 per month creates $600 to $900 in new monthly recurring revenue. If those members stay an average of 12 months, each one is worth $1,800 or more in lifetime value depending on your programming and retention.

The real question is whether the agency you're evaluating has the infrastructure to actually deliver that — or whether you're paying for access to someone's time and hoping for the best.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

WHAT DOES A GYM MARKETING AGENCY TYPICALLY CHARGE PER MONTH IN 2026?

Fees range from around $500 per month for single-service arrangements to $5,000 or more for full-funnel management. Most independent gym owners working with a full-service agency spend between $1,500 and $4,500 per month in agency fees, with ad spend budgeted separately on top of that.

IS AD SPEND INCLUDED IN A GYM MARKETING AGENCY'S MONTHLY FEE?

Usually not. Most reputable agencies bill ad spend separately from their management fee and require your approval before spending. Be cautious of any agency claiming to include meaningful ad spend inside a low monthly retainer without a clear explanation of the actual budget.

WHAT'S THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A GYM MARKETING COACHING PROGRAM AND A DONE-FOR-YOU AGENCY?

Coaching programs like Gym Launch or Two-Brain Business teach you how to market your gym. Done-for-you agencies execute the marketing for you. The right choice depends on whether you want to learn the skill or delegate it. Both have legitimate use cases — they're just not interchangeable.

WHAT SHOULD A GYM MARKETING AGENCY'S REPORTING LOOK LIKE?

At minimum, you should see ad performance, lead volume, and cost per lead on a regular basis. The best setups give you a live dashboard you can check anytime rather than waiting on a monthly report. Enoch Portal gives gym owners a real-time view of their lead pipeline, ad spend, roadmap, and delivery updates.

HOW LONG DOES IT TAKE TO SEE RESULTS FROM A GYM MARKETING AGENCY?

A realistic ramp period is 60 to 90 days. The first month typically involves setup, audience building, and initial testing. By the end of a 90-day cycle, you should have enough data to evaluate whether the strategy is working and what needs to be adjusted.

WHY DO SOME GYM MARKETING PROGRAMS CHARGE $18,000 TO $40,000?

Programs at that price point are coaching and consulting models, not execution services. You're paying for access to a system, curriculum, and mentorship — with the expectation that you or your team will implement the strategy. That's a fundamentally different product than an agency running the funnel for you.

WHAT'S A FAIR WAY TO EVALUATE GYM MARKETING AGENCY PRICING?

Compare what's included in the fee, not just the number. Confirm whether ad spend is separate, who owns the assets, what reporting looks like, and whether follow-up is automated or manual. Then calculate the revenue impact of consistent new-member acquisition against your total monthly investment — including ad spend — to decide whether the math works for your gym.

MAKE THE DECISION WITH FULL INFORMATION

Gym marketing agency pricing in 2026 runs a wide range because the category covers everything from single-service freelancers to full-funnel partners with proprietary technology. The fee you see in a proposal is rarely the whole story.

Before you sign anything, get clear on exactly what you're buying: execution or education, one channel or the full funnel, a live dashboard or a monthly PDF. Those distinctions matter far more than whether one agency charges $200 more per month than another.
