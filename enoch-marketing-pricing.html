Enoch Marketing runs on flat-rate monthly retainers. No hidden setup fees, no surprises buried in the contract. Ad spend is billed separately, and nothing moves until you approve it. Here's how the tiers break down:

What Each Plan Includes

Get Found: $1,500/month

This tier is for gyms that are essentially invisible in local search. If your Google Business Profile is incomplete, your website doesn't rank for anything in your city, and you're not showing up when someone searches "CrossFit gym near me," this is where you start.

Get Found covers local SEO and Google Business Profile management — optimizing your listing, building citation consistency, and creating the on-page signals that tell Google exactly where your gym operates. It also includes a mobile-first conversion website built for both traditional search and AI platforms like ChatGPT.

The goal is straightforward: get found before you try to scale paid acquisition.

Fill the Floor: $2,500/month

This plan adds paid advertising on Meta and Google to the local SEO foundation. Facebook and Instagram put you in front of people who aren't actively searching yet. Google captures the ones who are. Running both together means you're present at awareness and at the moment of decision.

Fill the Floor also includes AI-assisted email and SMS lead follow-up through Enoch Scout. When a lead comes in, Enoch Scout initiates contact, qualifies the conversation, and routes it toward an intro call. When a conversation requires real judgment, it escalates to your team — no automated reply that costs you the lead.

Done-for-you social content planning and publishing is included here too, which removes one of the most consistently time-consuming tasks gym owners end up doing themselves.

Growth Engine: $4,500/month

This is the full-funnel plan. Every service Enoch offers is active: local SEO, Google Business Profile, paid ads on Meta and Google, AI-assisted follow-up, social content, and a conversion website. The difference between Fill the Floor and Growth Engine is depth and bandwidth — more campaign variations, more content, more proactive optimization.

Clients at this tier also get access to Enoch Portal, the agency's private client dashboard. Your live roadmap, lead pipeline, ad spend, and delivery updates are all in one place. You're not waiting on a monthly report or chasing someone for a status update. The data is there when you want it.

Custom Growth: Custom Proposal

Built for multi-location operators or gym businesses with a scope that doesn't fit the standard tiers. Pricing is scoped through a proposal conversation. If you're running two or more locations and need unified reporting and coordinated campaigns, this is where that conversation starts.

What's Not Included — and Why That's a Good Thing

Ad spend is billed separately and requires your approval before anything is committed. Some agencies bundle media spend into a flat fee, which sounds cleaner but removes your visibility into how much is actually going to ads versus the agency's margin.

With Enoch, the retainer covers strategy, execution, creative, and management. Your ad spend goes directly to Meta and Google. Both numbers are visible.

Third-party software works the same way. If a tool is needed, it's disclosed and approved — not absorbed into the retainer and quietly marked up.

How to Think About ROI

The ROI question for gym marketing comes down to one number: what is a new member worth to your gym?

If your average member pays $150 per month and stays for 14 months, that's $2,100 in lifetime value per member. At Fill the Floor pricing of $2,500 per month, you need roughly two new members per month from marketing to cover the retainer — before ad spend.

The ROI Frame

That's not a guarantee. Enoch doesn't promise a specific number of new members or a specific revenue outcome, and any agency that does should raise a flag. What the math does is frame the question correctly. You're not asking "is $2,500 a lot?" You're asking "can this consistently generate more than two new members per month who wouldn't have found me otherwise?"

It's also worth running the comparison against what you're already spending. If a freelancer, a web developer, and a CRM are costing you $2,200 per month combined — and none of them are accountable to the same outcome — the retainer cost difference is smaller than it looks.

The Vendor Fragmentation Problem

Most gym owners at the plateau stage are managing three or four separate marketing relationships. A paid ads freelancer who doesn't touch the website. A web developer who doesn't know what the ads are saying. A CRM nobody configured for lead follow-up. Each vendor does their piece in isolation, and nobody owns the outcome when a lead comes in and doesn't convert.

Enoch's model is built around a single accountable partner. SEO, paid ads, website, lead follow-up, and content are all executed by the same team with visibility into the full funnel. When a paid ad drives a lead to a landing page and that lead doesn't convert, the same team that built the ad also built the page and manages the follow-up sequence. They can see where the breakdown happened and fix it.

That's not a marketing claim — it's a structural difference in how the service is delivered.

How Enoch Compares to Other Options

The gym marketing category is crowded, and it's worth being direct about what the alternatives actually are.

Gym Launch charges $18,000 to $40,000-plus in program fees and teaches gym owners how to market. It's a coaching and consulting model. Enoch executes the marketing for you. Those are fundamentally different products serving different owners at different stages.

Two-Brain Business is a 12-month mentorship model that reports an average first-year revenue increase of $23,013 for clients. That outcome comes from coaching owners to improve their own operations — not from an agency running campaigns on their behalf. Two-Brain doesn't manage paid ads, build websites, or run local SEO for clients.

The Distinction

If you want to learn to do it yourself, coaching programs have real value. If you want someone to run it while you coach classes, that's a different answer — and that's what Enoch does.

The 90-Day Growth Cycle

Every plan starts with a 90-day growth cycle before moving to month-to-month. This is a structured onboarding period, not a lock-in. The purpose is to give the strategy enough runway to produce meaningful data before any optimization decisions get made.

Paid campaigns need time to exit the learning phase. SEO changes need time to get indexed and move rankings. Follow-up sequences need enough lead volume to show conversion patterns. Ninety days is the minimum useful window for any of that to become visible.

After the 90-day cycle, the engagement continues month-to-month. No long-term contract holding you in.

Gym Recon: What's Coming

Gym Recon is a revenue intelligence tool currently in private beta. It's designed to surface billing risk, member churn signals, ad performance gaps, and prioritized recovery actions — giving gym owners a clearer picture of where revenue is quietly leaking before it becomes a visible problem.

It's not part of the current standard plans yet. But if you're evaluating Enoch as a long-term partner, it's worth knowing it's in development.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Enoch Marketing cost per month?
