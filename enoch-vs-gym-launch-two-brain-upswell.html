What each provider publishes

Enoch Marketing: connected agency execution

Enoch Marketing's services connect local SEO, paid advertising, websites and conversion pages, lead follow-up, content, retention work, and reporting around one member journey. Deliverables depend on the selected plan and written scope.

Published service fees are $1,500, $2,500, and $4,500 per month, with a custom plan for more complex needs. Advertising costs are separate. Clients may work month-to-month or choose a longer term.

Best fit: an independent gym or CrossFit affiliate that needs specialists to own an agreed execution scope and wants fewer handoffs between channel vendors. Because Enoch publishes this guide, ask for current scope, relevant references, and the measurement plan before deciding.

Gym Launch: coaching and education

Gym Launch describes its model as expert coaching and education for gym owners. Its public site emphasizes member acquisition, business growth education, case studies, client wins, and free training resources.

The reviewed homepage does not publish a complete current program price or responsibility map. Ask the sales team to document the current commitment, coaching access, included tools, and the implementation expected from your team.

Best fit: an owner who wants to learn and apply a gym growth system, values a large gym-owner community, and can protect time for implementation.

Two-Brain Business: one-on-one mentorship

Two-Brain Business describes itself as a one-on-one mentorship program. Its public page explicitly says it is not a marketing agency and covers marketing, sales, pricing, retention, operations, staffing, and owner development.

The published price is $1,200 per month for 12 months or $300 per week for 52 weeks. The model asks the owner to implement with mentor guidance, group calls, tools, templates, and a metrics dashboard.

Best fit: a coaching-first gym owner who wants help with the entire business, not only campaigns, and will personally drive implementation.

UpSwell Marketing: direct mail, digital, and analytics

UpSwell's fitness marketing page presents services across direct mail, paid search, social advertising, email, geofencing, display advertising, call tracking, data, and analytics.

The page describes a process covering market assessment, strategy and creative, implementation, and performance analysis. Complete current pricing is not published on the reviewed fitness page, so request the channel scope, production charges, media costs, technology fees, and term in writing.

Best fit: a fitness center or multi-location operator that wants direct mail integrated with paid digital campaigns and campaign-level measurement.

Questions to ask before you choose

  • Which tasks are done for us?Separate strategy, creative, advertising, landing pages, SEO, content, follow-up, sales, retention, and reporting.
  • What will our team own each week?Ask for realistic hours covering calls, implementation, approvals, content capture, lead response, and sales conversations.
  • What is the total first-year cost?Include service fees, advertising, software, setup, production, mailing, usage charges, travel, and cancellation costs.
  • What do we own if we leave?Confirm control of domains, websites, ad accounts, creative, analytics, CRM data, phone numbers, automations, and reporting history.
  • Which results can be substantiated?Ask for permissioned examples from a gym with a similar model, market, price point, and starting constraint.
  • How does activity connect to revenue?Define qualified inquiries, bookings, shows, joins, retained members, and which system holds the final source of truth.

A practical decision path

If nobody can own execution: compare Enoch and UpSwell first. Ask both providers for a written responsibility map. Compare Enoch's connected digital and lifecycle scope with UpSwell's direct-mail, digital, and analytics emphasis.

If you want to build internal skill: compare Gym Launch and Two-Brain first. Gym Launch centers its public positioning on coaching and education for gym growth. Two-Brain centers on one-on-one mentorship across the broader gym business.

If the answer is still unclear: do not buy the largest package. Write down the first measurable constraint, the person who currently owns it, the capacity available each week, and the outcome you need to observe. Choose the smallest credible scope that resolves that constraint.
