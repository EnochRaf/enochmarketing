When someone searches "CrossFit gym near me," your Google Business Profile can shape what they see before they visit your website. A complete profile cannot guarantee a local pack position, but it can help Google understand the business and help a prospect decide whether to call, request directions, or book.

A gym can have a polished website and still lose local-search clicks because its hours are wrong, its category is vague, or its profile gives a prospect no clear next step. The opposite can also happen: a modest website can be supported by an accurate profile with useful photos, candid reviews, and a direct booking link.

Google Business Profile supplies information that can appear across Google Search and Maps, including local results with a map, ratings, photos, hours, and contact actions. Claiming and maintaining the profile is free, although the staff time, photography, and systems required to manage it are real operating costs.

This article is a comprehensive playbook for treating your GBP the way the gyms winning the local pack actually treat theirs, as a live, maintained marketing asset, not a one-time form you filled out when you opened.

The Reality Check

"Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence. You can improve the information and experience you control, but no checklist can guarantee a specific rank."

Before you improve the profile, understand what each element communicates to the person viewing it and whether the information is accurate enough for Google to match it to relevant searches. Start with policy and clarity, then measure the actions the profile produces.

The Review Strategy That Actually Compounds

The review system should make it straightforward for customers to share genuine experiences, including experiences that were not perfect. Google allows businesses to ask for reviews, but it prohibits incentives, pressure, and selectively asking only customers expected to leave positive feedback.

Policy Guardrail

Do not ask, "Were you happy? If yes, review us here." That is review gating. Send the same neutral invitation to a broad, defined customer group and let the reviewer choose the rating and content.

The Review Ask System

Build a repeatable request into a neutral lifecycle point, such as a 30-day check-in sent to every eligible member. Keep the request separate from incentives, contests, refunds, and staff quotas. Your target is fair request coverage and useful feedback, not a predetermined number of five-star reviews.

Neutral request: "Hi [Name], we ask members at this point in their experience for honest feedback. If you would like to share yours on Google, here is the direct link: [review link]. There is no obligation, and candid feedback helps prospective members make an informed choice."

Response standard: Thank the reviewer, address the point they raised, and keep personal account details private. If the issue is sensitive, acknowledge it briefly and invite the person to a private channel. Do not add health details, membership history, or promotional keywords to the reply.

GBP Posts That Give Prospects a Useful Next Step

Business Profile posts can share updates, offers, and event details on Search and Maps. Google says posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set. A weekly cadence is an Enoch operating recommendation for gyms with enough useful material, not a published ranking requirement. Start with posts that answer a question or send the reader to one measurable next step.

The Monthly GBP Maintenance Calendar

Optimising the profile once is not enough because hours, links, staff, services, and offers change. This recurring routine keeps customer-facing information accurate and gives the team a consistent way to learn from profile actions. Adjust the cadence to the volume of changes and enquiries at your gym.

Monthly GBP Maintenance Calendar
Spread across 4 weeks, never more than 20 minutes per week once the habits are built
Week 1 Review & Respond
  • Respond to new reviews with a two-business-day internal service goal, while protecting reviewer privacy
  • Check Q&A section for any new user-submitted questions, answer all of them before they sit unanswered
  • Review any suggested edits from Google or users, accept accurate ones, flag and reject incorrect ones
  • Post your first weekly "What's New" update, what happened in class this week
Week 2 Photos & Content
  • Upload 5–8 new photos from the past two weeks, mix of class action, community moments, and at least one coach photo
  • Post your monthly Offer post, free trial, current promotion, or challenge sign-up
  • Post a member story or testimonial post with a booking CTA
  • Check that your hours are accurate, public holidays, special event days, schedule changes
Week 3 Ask & Grow
  • Send the same neutral review invitation to the full eligible cohort defined by your policy, without incentives or sentiment screening
  • Post a FAQ/education post addressing the objection you've heard most from prospects this month
  • Check your profile's Insights in the GBP dashboard, note search query trends and photo view counts
  • If you have an upcoming event or challenge, create an Event post with a registration link
Week 4 Audit & Compete
  • Search "CrossFit near me" and "CrossFit [your city]", note who appears in the local pack ahead of you and what their profiles have that yours doesn't
  • Review public competitor profiles for customer-experience gaps you can address honestly, without treating review counts as a ranking formula
  • Review your Services section, have you added or changed anything this month that should be reflected?
  • Check GBP for newly available features or attributes, Google adds new options regularly without notification
Planning estimate: 60 to 90 minutes per month, depending on review volume, events, and profile changes. Track time alongside qualified actions so the cadence earns its place.

Build the Profile Operating System

Profile Completion, Do This Week
  • Open the profile and verify the business name, address, phone, website, booking link, regular hours, and special hours
  • Audit the primary category and use the fewest accurate additional categories needed to describe the core business
  • Rewrite your business description with your city name, who you're for, and what makes you different, 500–700 characters, first-person, specific
  • Activate every attribute available in your category, work through the full list in GBP and toggle on everything accurate
  • Add all your services individually with descriptions, group classes, foundations, open gym, personal training, nutrition coaching, specialty courses
  • Review customer questions and answer them factually, with clear information about the first visit, pricing structure, and accessibility
  • Upload a useful starter set of current photos covering the exterior, entrance, facility, coaches, class flow, and beginner scaling, with permission where needed
  • Respond to unanswered reviews when a reply would be helpful, keeping private account and health information out of public responses
Ongoing System, Build the Habit
  • Set a recurring calendar block to publish a useful update or offer when you have one, using a tagged link to measure response
  • Build a neutral review invitation into a defined lifecycle point for every eligible member, with no incentive or positive-sentiment screen
  • Assign media updates to a trained team member and require permission records for identifiable member content
  • Turn on available profile notifications and set a separate recurring check so questions, edits, and reviews do not depend on one alert
  • Check GBP Insights monthly: which search queries drove the most views? Which photos got the most views? Use this data to inform what content you create next month.
  • Run the Week 4 market review monthly and use it to improve factual information, offer clarity, and the booking experience

The Bottom Line

A well-maintained Google Business Profile can be an efficient local discovery channel because claiming it does not require media spend. Its actual cost per qualified lead will vary by market, visibility, review profile, offer, and measurement quality, so compare it with your paid and referral channels using the same lead definitions.

You cannot control distance or force a local pack position. You can control whether the profile is accurate, whether categories follow policy, whether review requests are neutral, whether photos reduce uncertainty, whether posts have a clear next step, and whether every link is measured.

Start with accuracy and policy, then improve the prospect experience. Review qualified calls, directions, website visits, and bookings monthly so the channel is judged by business outcomes, not rank alone.

Open the profile today and fix the first inaccurate or unclear detail a prospect would see.
